The question

How does a mountain change what we see?

This study reconstructs the view from Dale Head using genuine elevation data. The summit mode is a calculated 360° landscape silhouette, with near and distant ridges layered by their measured heights and distances. The separate 3D overview lets you explore the terrain from above. Neither is a photograph or a claim to reproduce trees, buildings or the atmosphere.

Reading the map

Terrain first, then visibility

The summit panorama is computed from the height grid and the documented 753 m summit elevation; the 3D map is an elevated terrain study rather than a literal eye-level camera. When the independently generated GDAL data are available, Visible ground displays a calculated terrain-only viewshed: green is visible and grey is obscured by intervening land. If computation is unavailable, that control remains disabled rather than presenting invented visibility.

On touch screens, drag to look around. Use the direction buttons to return to a known heading, or switch to 3D overview for a conventional map.
