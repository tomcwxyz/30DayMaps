The ambition

England wants everyone to be within a 15-minute walk of green or blue space. In 2025, only 80% of households met that commitment. What would it take to close the gaps?

That national statistic includes parks, watersides and other spaces. The local maps below show woodland alone, using a different, simpler proximity test.

Read this carefully

A proposition, not a proposal.

These aren't ten-minute walking maps. They show 800 m in a straight line from the edge of woodland with indicated public access — a deliberately simple proximity proxy. Each 250 m grid square represents a sample location, not a household, person or walking route. A changed square means possible proximity, not proven access.

Candidate woodlands are selected algorithmically from nearby woods with no known public access to show the largest reduction in gap squares in this map. That doesn't mean access could be granted, or that a new entrance or path exists. Woodland data has not been ground-truthed. The actual national 15-minute walking statistic uses much richer data.
