The proposition

Not another small improvement.

Rather than proposing one new park, imagine making nature a defining purpose of the whole region. The western uplands, river corridors, old railway alignments and existing woods help shape the map — but the change itself is explicitly imagined.

01 / Make space for a wild core

Prioritise a more continuous nature-first landscape towards the Pennines, rather than scattering isolated pockets.

02 / Give rivers room

Use actual Tyne, Wear and Tees alignments to favour land next to waterways. The 450 m buffer is a scenario rule, not a modelled floodplain.

03 / Reconnect the landscape

Give greater weight to land near existing woods and disused railway alignments. These lines are mapped infrastructure, not verified greenways.

04 / Keep the cities

Existing built-up cells are protected from reallocation in the model. The transformation concerns how the wider landscape might work around settlements.

How this map was made

The future is imagined.
The inputs are not.

Existing land cover comes from ESA WorldCover 2021, sampled at 300 m for this regional illustration. The North East boundary is from the Office for National Statistics. Named waterways and former railway alignments come from OpenStreetMap contributors.

The proposed 2050 areas follow a reproducible scoring rule: priority to western land, proximity to existing tree/wetland cover, and proximity to mapped rivers and former railways. The highest-scoring undeveloped terrestrial cells are added until the nature-first allocation covers 50% of the sampled land. The map makes no claim about land ownership, ecological condition, rights of access, restoration cost, permissions, agricultural trade-offs or feasibility.
